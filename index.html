BBC Player

Platform Migration & UX Redesign · Singapore & Malaysia
+150% MAU Growth
S$500K Migration Budget

The Problem

BBC Player was a premium British content streaming service for paid TV customers in Singapore and Malaysia. By the time I was brought in, the platform was drowning in technical debt.

⚠️ Chronic P1 Issues

6–8 priority-one incidents per week, consuming the entire team's capacity and preventing any meaningful product development.

🏗️ Technical Debt

Accumulated system debt made the platform increasingly unstable. Patches were creating new problems faster than they fixed old ones.

🏁 Competitive Gap

Competitors had moved far ahead on UX, content discovery, and metadata quality. The service could not compete in its current state.

My Role

Post budget approval, I was brought in as business & operations SME embedded with the product team. My ownership spanned three areas:

01 UX & Journey Design

• Wireframed sign-in screens and core user flows
• Ensured edge cases and error states were accounted for
• Contributed to journey maps shared with engineering

02 Metadata Logic

• Designed metadata framework balancing licensing, editorial & user needs
• Activated underutilised CMS fields (short description, tagging)
• Built collections feature to address editorial curation requests

03 Business Prioritisation

• Co-decided feature scope with Senior PM against North Star
• Represented business perspective in trade-off decisions
• Coordinated UAT and sign-off process for launch

Technical Depth — Metadata Logic

One of the most complex pieces I owned was designing the metadata framework. Three distinct problems, one coherent solution.

LICENSING · Channel Attribution Requirement

TV licensing agreements required explicit channel name association for every piece of content. I encoded this as a mandatory metadata field, ensuring legal compliance without cluttering the user interface.

TRUST · Content Expiry Visibility

Users had no way of knowing when content was leaving the platform, eroding trust. I designed 'leaving soon' metadata logic so users could plan their viewing — a simple field that had outsized impact on retention.

DISCOVERY · Rich Metadata & Collections

Thin metadata forced decisions from one thumbnail. I activated unused CMS fields and designed a tagging system letting editors group content into user-facing collections.

HOW: Competitive analysis during 7-week UX apprenticeship → drafted logic framework → validated with BBC UX teams and editorial stakeholders against GEL guidelines and licensing requirements → implemented via CMS field activation + tagging system with engineers.

Prioritisation & Trade-offs

NORTH STAR: Ship a stable, compliant, competitive platform within Y2019 budget — prioritise structural reliability over feature richness.

✗ DROPPED
  • Data analytics enrichment (assumption-based decisions accepted as a known trade-off)
  • Marketing tool integrations
  • Sophisticated content recommendation interface
✓ BUILT
  • Simple but highly flexible Lego-block content curation system
  • Metadata logic framework (licensing, expiry, discovery)
  • SSO/OAuth entitlement integration for clean access control
  • Tagging and collections for editorial flexibility

Outcome & Reflection

+150% User Acquisition
+300% Engagement
9 Months to Deliver
WHAT I'D DO DIFFERENTLY

The analytics trade-off is one I still think about. We migrated basic tracking as-is, but the team was still making learned assumptions — amalgamating data from multiple sources to understand drop-off, content consumption patterns, and next-episode behaviour.

I pushed for discovery work on enriched analytics but it was overruled given the tight budget and timeline. In hindsight, I'd have scoped something far leaner — even basic event tracking on two or three key user actions would have transformed how we prioritised the post-launch roadmap. The lesson: when you can't do the full thing, advocate for the smallest version that still gives you signal.

The Problem

CGW offered materially better FX rates and multi-currency functionality — but adoption was far below potential. The instinct was to assume a product problem. The reality was different.

THE SIGNAL: A surge in frontline enquiries and guerrilla testing with internal staff revealed customers weren't asking how to improve CGW — they were asking what it was, where to find it, and whether it was even a debit feature.

THE GUERRILLA TEST

During holiday season, we asked internal staff across branch, digital, and marketing to use CGW while travelling — verbatim: "use your CGW, we want to see if our rates are competitive." Branch staff — the people selling this product to customers daily — came back with "what is CGW?" Digital knew because they built it. Marketing knew because they promoted it. Branch didn't.

💡 KEY REFRAME: The problem was not product quality — it was pre-product. Customers couldn't evaluate CGW because they didn't know it existed or understand what it was. This changed everything about the solution.

Three Segments, Three Different Problems

Discovery revealed customers weren't one homogeneous group. Each was blocked at a different point — requiring a different solution.

01 EXISTING USERS · Had CGW. Forgot to use it.

PROBLEM: Had CGW enabled but forgot to turn on overseas usage or were unaware of features like auto top-up.

SOLUTION: Surface overseas toggle and auto top-up prominently within a consolidated CGW settings view.

02 INACTIVE CUSTOMERS · Eligible. Didn't know.

PROBLEM: Held eligible debit cards but didn't realise CGW was a debit feature or how to activate it.

SOLUTION: Onboarding screens and persistent in-app tooltips at high-intent moments to educate and activate.

03 NEW-TO-BANK · Never heard of it.

PROBLEM: Unaware CGW existed but curious once they encountered the value proposition.

SOLUTION: Social deeplinks and homepage placement surfacing CGW during onboarding journey.

What We Built — And Why

NORTH STAR: Feature visibility first — make CGW findable, understandable, and activatable before investing in richer functionality.

✗ CONSIDERED & DROPPED
  • Homepage module (homepage estate not ours to use — regulatory & ownership constraints)
  • Rate comparison charts vs treasury (technically infeasible within sprint capacity; also premature)
  • Comprehensive rewards overhaul (Singapore is a credit card country — debit incentivisation requires separate budget)
✓ BUILT
  • Consolidated CGW hub (single self-serve view combining currency conversion, eFX, price alerts, overseas toggle, card settings and balance)
  • Intent-based activation funnel (education → intent → activation at receptive moments)
  • In-app signals over direct comms (regulatory constraints prevented direct promotional messaging)

The Harder Constraint — Playing the Long Game

The solution wasn't just constrained by tech sprints. It was constrained by organisational reality — and navigating that was as important as the product decisions.

🏦 Debit is not the bank's priority

Senior leadership viewed CGW through an eFX revenue lens, not a transaction volume lens. Every decision had to ladder up to that framing to stay funded and visible.

⚖️ A big swing could kill the product

Requesting a large budget for an unproven overhaul required going to project forums, passing compliance reviews, and competing with higher-priority initiatives. A failed swing wouldn't just waste money — it would lose the mandate entirely.

📈 Show incremental value to keep it alive

We were playing the long game. Small, defensible wins would keep CGW funded and on the roadmap. This shaped every prioritisation decision — not timidity, but strategic patience.

Outcome — Why 10% Is The Right Number

~10% Activation Uplift
100% Clean MAS Audit
3 Segments Targeted
WHY THIS NUMBER IS DEFENSIBLE

Baseline: Quarterly cashback campaigns — where we literally give customers money back — move activation in single digits. Users activate to receive cashback, then drop off immediately.

Context: We offered no cashback incentive here. We asked users to change behaviour based on understanding alone, in a credit card country where debit products are inherently harder to activate.

Conclusion: 10% without incentives, with durable behavioural change rather than a temporary spike, against a single-digit baseline with incentives — this is a meaningful result, not a conservative target.

Note: The S$6M FX revenue achievement came from broader debit card product positioning work across my tenure, not solely from this CGW activation project.
