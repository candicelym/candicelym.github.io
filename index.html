BBC Player Platform Migration & UX Redesign

Singapore & Malaysia | 2021–2022

The Problem

BBC Player was drowning in technical debt with 6–8 P1 incidents per week consuming the entire team's capacity. The platform was increasingly unstable, and competitors had moved far ahead on UX, content discovery, and metadata quality.

My Role

Brought in as business & operations SME embedded with the product team, I owned three critical areas:

  • UX & Journey Design: Wireframed sign-in screens and core user flows, ensuring edge cases and error states were accounted for
  • Metadata Logic: Designed metadata framework balancing licensing requirements, editorial needs, and user discovery
  • Business Prioritisation: Co-decided feature scope with Senior PM, represented business perspective in trade-off decisions, and coordinated UAT

Technical Depth: Metadata Framework

One of the most complex pieces I owned was designing the metadata framework to solve three distinct problems:

  • Licensing: Encoded mandatory channel attribution to ensure legal compliance without cluttering UI
  • Trust: Designed 'leaving soon' metadata logic so users could plan viewing—simple field with outsized retention impact
  • Discovery: Activated unused CMS fields and designed tagging system for editorial content collections

Prioritisation & Trade-offs

North Star: Ship a stable, compliant, competitive platform within budget—prioritise structural reliability over feature richness.

Built: Simple but flexible content curation system, metadata logic framework, SSO/OAuth integration, tagging and collections

Dropped: Data analytics enrichment (accepted as known trade-off), marketing tool integrations, sophisticated recommendation interface

Outcome & Reflection

Results: +150% user acquisition, +300% engagement, delivered in 9 months

What I'd Do Differently: The analytics trade-off is one I still think about. We migrated basic tracking as-is, but the team was making learned assumptions. I pushed for enriched analytics but was overruled given tight budget and timeline. In hindsight, I'd have scoped something leaner—even basic event tracking on 2-3 key actions would have transformed post-launch prioritisation. The lesson: when you can't do the full thing, advocate for the smallest version that still gives you signal.

Citi Global Wallet Activation & Adoption

Debit Card Product | Singapore | 2022–2024

The Problem

Citi Global Wallet (CGW) offered materially better FX rates and multi-currency functionality, but adoption was far below potential. The instinct was to assume a product problem. The reality was different.

A surge in frontline enquiries and guerrilla testing with internal staff revealed customers weren't asking how to improve CGW—they were asking what it was, where to find it, and whether it was even a debit feature.

Key Reframe: The problem wasn't product quality—it was pre-product. Customers couldn't evaluate CGW because they didn't know it existed. This changed everything about the solution.

Three Segments, Three Different Problems

Discovery revealed customers weren't homogeneous. Each segment was blocked at a different point:

  • Existing Users: Had CGW but forgot to use it—needed overseas toggle and auto top-up surfaced prominently
  • Inactive Customers: Eligible but didn't know CGW was a debit feature—needed onboarding screens and in-app tooltips at high-intent moments
  • New-to-Bank: Never heard of it but curious once they saw value prop—needed social deeplinks and homepage placement

What We Built—and Why

North Star: Feature visibility first—make CGW findable, understandable, and activatable before investing in richer functionality.

Built:

  • Consolidated CGW hub combining currency conversion, eFX, price alerts, overseas toggle, card settings
  • Intent-based activation funnel targeting moments when users were most receptive
  • In-app signals over direct comms (regulatory constraints prevented promotional messaging)

Considered & Dropped: Homepage module (regulatory constraints), rate comparison charts (technically infeasible), comprehensive rewards overhaul (separate budget track required)

The Harder Constraint—Playing the Long Game

The solution wasn't just constrained by tech sprints—it was constrained by organisational reality:

  • Debit is not the bank's priority: Leadership viewed CGW through an eFX revenue lens, not transaction volume. Every decision had to ladder up to that framing to stay funded.
  • A big swing could kill the product: Requesting large budget required passing compliance reviews and competing with higher-priority initiatives. A failed swing wouldn't just waste money—it would lose the mandate entirely.
  • Show incremental value to keep it alive: Small, defensible wins would keep CGW funded. This shaped every prioritisation decision—not timidity, but strategic patience.

Outcome—Why 10% is the Right Number

Results: ~10% activation uplift, S$6M FX revenue (3-year high), 100% clean MAS audit

Why This Number is Defensible:

Baseline: Quarterly cashback campaigns—where we literally give customers money—move activation in single digits. Users activate to receive cashback, then drop off immediately.

Context: We offered no cashback incentive. We asked users to change behaviour based on understanding alone, in a credit card country where debit products are inherently harder to activate.

Conclusion: 10% without incentives, with durable behavioural change rather than temporary spike, against a single-digit baseline with incentives—this is a meaningful result, not a conservative target.
